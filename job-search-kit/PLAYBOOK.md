# Running the Job Search Week to Week

## The rhythm

| When | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Digest arrives (every 2 days) | Scan the list, open the 2–5 that interest you | 10–15 min |
| Same day | Apply with a base CV, or make a quick per-job version for the ones you really want | 15–30 min per job |
| Weekly | Adjust your profile if the digest is noisy or thin | 10 min |
| 2–3 days before an interview | Run interview prep, rehearse out loud | 60–90 min |

Apply quickly. Good roles often close within 2–3 weeks, sometimes sooner.

## Reading the digest

Each job shows: company, title, location, salary (disclosed or **estimated — verify**),
company health, your strongest match, your biggest gap, **which base CV to use**, and the link.

- **"Apply to these"** at the bottom lists verified-open jobs only. Work from that list.
- **"Could not verify"** jobs may already be closed. Check the company's own careers page first.
- **Company health** (Strong / Moderate / Weak / Can't assess) is a quick read on whether a
  startup looks stable: funding, layoffs, growth. It is a signal, not a guarantee.
- A job that has been sent once is never sent again. If you want to revisit one, search your
  Claude Gmail account's sent folder.

Keep track of what you applied to: a simple Google Sheet (company, role, date, CV used,
status) is enough. Or star the job in the digest email.

## Base CV or per-job version

| Use the base CV as it is when… | Make a per-job version when… |
|---|---|
| The digest's "Use: CV_X" fits well | You really want this job |
| The posting is standard for your role family | The posting stresses something your base CV underplays |
| You are applying to many similar roles | The role sits between two of your role families |

Per-job version: new chat, **"Tailor my CV to this job"** + link. Claude changes only the title
line, profile and key competencies, so it takes 10–15 minutes, not an hour.

Most German application portals want **PDF**. Open the CV in Google Docs → File → Download →
PDF. If a posting asks for an **Anschreiben** (cover letter), ask Claude for one. Keep it to
one page and specific to the posting.

## Tuning the search

Change the **profile**, never the scheduled task. Either edit the Google Doc directly or
tell Claude "Update my profile: …". The next run uses it automatically.

| Symptom | Likely fix |
|---|---|
| Too many irrelevant jobs | Make hard limits more precise; remove a vague role family; add sectors to "ruled out" |
| Too few jobs | Move some hard limits to preferences; add German titles (e.g. "Projektleiter", "Referent", "Sachbearbeiter"); widen commute by 15 minutes; allow one seniority level up and down |
| Salaries look too low | Check the salary floor against the market. Ask Claude for current ranges with sources |
| Jobs from a company you never want | Add it to the **Blocklist** section |
| Too many emails | Change frequency in the profile, then "change my job search schedule" |

## Interviews

- Start prep 2–3 days before, not the night before. Claude's research takes a few minutes;
  rehearsing takes longer.
- Tell Claude **who** is interviewing you. An HR screen, a hiring manager and a technical panel
  need different preparation.
- **Say your answers out loud.** Type them to Claude for feedback, then say them aloud a few times.
  An answer that reads well can still sound stiff.
- Practise **"Why are you looking?"** until it is calm and short. One true sentence about what
  happened, no blame, then what you want next. Interviewers mainly check that you are not
  bitter and that you know what you want.
- Bring the prep sheet. Use the "Questions to ask them" section. Asking good questions is
  half of how interviewers judge you.

## What Claude gets wrong, and what to check

- **Closed jobs.** Claude checks, but postings close daily. If a link is dead, move on.
- **Salary estimates.** Undisclosed salaries are estimates from public data. Treat them as
  a range to verify, never as fact.
- **Fit.** Claude can be optimistic. Read the posting's must-haves yourself.
- **CV facts.** Read every line of every CV. Claude must not invent anything, but you are the
  one who signs it. Wrong dates or inflated titles can cost you an offer later.
- **Company research.** News can be outdated or about a different company with a similar name.
  Click the sources before you mention something in an interview.
- **Your own judgement.** The digest ranks jobs; it does not know your network, your instinct or
  what you heard from a friend at that company.

## What works best, from experience with this setup

- **The scheduled search finds jobs you would not find yourself**, mostly on company career
  pages and smaller job systems that never reach the big boards. Let it run, even when you
  are also searching manually.
- **2–4 base CVs beat rewriting for every job.** Full rewrites per application took too long.
  Strong base CVs plus an occasional quick edit is the sweet spot.
- **Company research and good questions matter more than anything else in interview prep.**
  They show genuine interest and give you what you need to decide.
- **A separate Gmail account keeps things clean.** Your private inbox stays out of it, and
  it doubles as the memory of what has already been sent.

## What the kit cannot do for you

- **Referrals.** A former colleague who passes your CV to the hiring manager beats any
  application. Tell your network what you are looking for, specifically: titles, cities,
  sectors. Ask Claude to help you write that message.
- **Recruiter calls.** Pick up, be clear about your salary floor and your limits.
- **Deciding.** When an offer comes, ask Claude to help you compare it against your profile.
  The decision is yours.

## When you sign

1. Pause or delete the scheduled task (sidebar → **Scheduled**).
2. Cancel the Claude subscription if you no longer need it.
3. Delete the Claude Gmail account if you want your data gone.
4. Pass this kit on to someone who needs it.
